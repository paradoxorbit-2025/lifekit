Right to Equality
▼
📜
Article 14–18

What It Means

Every Indian citizen is equal before the law. No one can be discriminated against based on religion, caste, sex, place of birth, or race — in government services, public places, or employment.

✓

What You Can Do

If denied service or discriminated in public place — file complaint with District Magistrate, Human Rights Commission, or NHRC (nhrc.nic.in).

🗣️
Right to Freedom of Speech
▼
📜
Article 19(1)(a)

What It Means

You have the right to express opinions, criticize government, protest peacefully, and speak freely — subject to reasonable restrictions on public order and decency.

✓

Limits

Hate speech, incitement to violence, and defamation are not protected. Free speech does not include harassing individuals.

📚
Right to Education (RTE)
▼
📜
Article 21A + RTE Act 2009

What It Means

Every child aged 6–14 has the right to free and compulsory education in a government school. No school can deny admission, charge fees, or expel a child in this age group.

✓

25% Reservation

Private schools must reserve 25% seats for economically weaker sections (EWS). Apply through state RTE portal — it's free.

✓

If Denied

Complain to Block Education Officer or District Magistrate. School can face heavy penalties for refusing admission.

🛡️
Right to Life & Personal Liberty
▼
📜
Article 21

Most Powerful Right

Article 21 is the broadest fundamental right — it includes right to live with dignity, right to privacy, right to education, right to health, and protection from arbitrary state action.

✓

Medical Emergency

Under Article 21, NO hospital — government or private — can deny emergency medical treatment due to inability to pay. Treatment first, payment later.

✓

Enforce It

If Article 21 is violated — file a Writ Petition directly in High Court. This right is directly enforceable against the State.

🕌
Freedom of Religion
▼
📜
Article 25–28

What It Means

Every person has the right to freely profess, practice, and propagate any religion. No one can be forced to pay taxes for promotion of another religion.

✓

Limits

Religious practices that endanger public order, morality, or health are not protected. Forcible conversion is illegal in several states.

🌿
Right to Clean Environment
▼
📜
Article 21 + Article 48A

What It Means

Supreme Court has held that right to clean environment, clean water, and pollution-free air is part of the Right to Life under Article 21.

✓

How to Complain

Pollution complaints: National Green Tribunal (NGT) at ngtnational.in — free to file. No lawyer needed for basic complaints. NGT has strong powers to fine industries.

👮
Rights Against Illegal Arrest
▼
📜
Article 22 + BNSS 2023

Your 5 Core Rights on Arrest

1. Right to know reason for arrest. 2. Right to be produced before Magistrate within 24 hours. 3. Right to inform a family member/friend. 4. Right to remain silent. 5. Right to a lawyer immediately.

✓

If Illegally Detained

File Habeas Corpus petition in High Court — fastest legal remedy to secure release from illegal detention. Any citizen can file it.

✓

Warrantless Arrest

Police can arrest without warrant for cognizable offences (serious crimes). For non-cognizable offences — they must get a warrant from Magistrate first.

⚠️

If police refuse to inform your family or allow lawyer access — that's a violation of your fundamental rights. Remember: you can stay silent until your lawyer arrives.

🤐
Right to Silence
▼
📜
Article 20(3)

No Self-Incrimination

You cannot be compelled to be a witness against yourself. You have the right to refuse to answer questions that may incriminate you.

✓

How to Use It

Simply say: "I exercise my right to remain silent and wish to speak to a lawyer." You don't need to explain further. Don't sign any statement without lawyer present.

✓

Confession to Police is Not Evidence

Under Indian law, a confession made to a police officer is NOT admissible as evidence. Only confessions to a Magistrate are valid.

📋
FIR Filing Rights
▼
1

Police MUST Register FIR

For cognizable offences (theft, rape, murder, fraud), police are legally obligated to register FIR immediately. They cannot refuse or ask you to come back later.

2

If Police Refuse

Send a written complaint by speed post to Superintendent of Police (SP). If still refused — file complaint directly before Magistrate under Section 175 BNSS.

3

Online FIR

Most states allow online FIR for certain offences. Check your state police website. Also file cybercrime at cybercrime.gov.in.

4

Get FIR Copy Free

You have the right to receive a free copy of your FIR immediately upon registration. Police cannot charge any fee for this.

🔓
Right to Bail
▼
1

Bailable Offences

You have a right to bail as a matter of right for bailable offences. Police cannot deny bail for minor offences — it's mandatory.

2

Non-Bailable Offences

Bail is at court's discretion. Apply to Sessions Court if Magistrate refuses. High Court has power to grant bail in all cases.

3

Default Bail

If police don't file chargesheet within 60–90 days of arrest (depending on offence), you have a statutory right to bail — courts must grant it.

🏠
Right Against Illegal Search
▼
1

Search Warrant Required

Police cannot search your home without a search warrant from a Magistrate — except in specific urgent circumstances (hot pursuit, etc.).

2

Demand to See Warrant

Ask to see the warrant before allowing entry. Note the warrant number, issuing Magistrate, and scope of search.

3

Witness Your Search

During search — two independent witnesses (panchas) must be present. Demand a copy of the seizure memo for everything taken.

Right to Free Legal Aid
▼
📜
Article 39A + Legal Services Authorities Act

Who Gets Free Legal Aid

Women, children, SC/ST, persons with disabilities, victims of trafficking, those with annual income below ₹3 lakh — are entitled to free government lawyer.

✓

How to Get It

Contact District Legal Services Authority (DLSA) in your district. Every court also has a Legal Aid desk — walk in and ask. It's completely free.

✓

NALSA Helpline

National Legal Services Authority: Call 15100 (free) for legal help. They provide lawyers, mediation, and guidance.

🚫
Protection Against Torture & Custodial Violence
▼
📜
Article 21 + IPC Sections

Custodial Violence is Illegal

Any physical or mental torture in police or judicial custody is illegal. Police officers who commit it face criminal charges and departmental action.

✓

How to Complain

File complaint with: NHRC (nhrc.nic.in | 14433), State Human Rights Commission, or SP of District. You can also petition High Court directly.

✓

Medical Exam Right

Any person in custody has the right to be medically examined — especially after alleging violence. Demand this right loudly and clearly.

⏱️
Right to Speedy Trial
▼
📜
Article 21 — SC Interpretation

What It Means

Supreme Court has held that unreasonable delay in trial violates Article 21. You can apply to High Court for expedited trial or bail if case is delayed without reason.

✓

Undertrial Rights

If you've been in jail as undertrial for half the maximum sentence for the alleged offence — you have the right to bail under Section 479 BNSS.

Consumer Rights
▼
📜
Consumer Protection Act 2019

Your 6 Consumer Rights

1. Right to Safety. 2. Right to Information. 3. Right to Choose. 4. Right to be Heard. 5. Right to Redressal. 6. Right to Consumer Education.

✓

File a Complaint

Online at consumerhelpline.gov.in or call 1915 (National Consumer Helpline). For claims under ₹50 lakh: District Consumer Commission. Free to file, no lawyer mandatory.

✓

E-Commerce Rights

Online sellers must display all charges upfront. No hidden charges. Return and refund policies must be clearly stated. You can complain to NCH for online fraud too.

💡

Always keep bills, warranty cards, and screenshots of online orders. These are your evidence in consumer court.

🏥
Right to Medical Care
▼
1

No Emergency Denial

NO hospital — government or private — can deny emergency medical treatment. Demanding payment before treatment in emergency = illegal. Treat first, pay later.

2

Right to Information

You have the right to know your diagnosis, treatment options, risks, and alternatives in language you understand before giving consent.

3

Medical Records

You have the right to receive copies of all your medical records, test reports, and discharge summary. Hospitals cannot refuse this.

4

Complaint for Medical Negligence

File with State Medical Council, National Medical Commission (nmc.org.in), or Consumer Court for compensation.

🏘️
Tenant Rights (Kirayedari Adhikar)
▼
1

No Arbitrary Eviction

Landlord cannot evict you without proper legal notice and reason. Most state tenancy laws require 1–3 months notice. Forced eviction without court order is illegal.

2

Rent Agreement is Key

Always get a written and registered rent agreement. Unregistered agreements still have legal value as evidence but registered ones are stronger.

3

Security Deposit

Landlord must return security deposit within 30 days of vacating (with deductions for legitimate damage). Document condition of house on move-in with photos.

4

If Harassed

Cutting off water/electricity to force eviction is illegal. File complaint with Rent Control Court or District Magistrate.

📄
Right to Information (RTI)
▼
📜
RTI Act 2005

What You Can Ask

Any citizen can ask any government department for any public information. Why your application is delayed, government expenses, tender details, officer's qualifications — anything.

✓

How to File

Online at rtionline.gov.in — ₹10 application fee. Reply must come within 30 days. Free for BPL card holders. No need for lawyer or reason.

✓

If Denied

File First Appeal with senior officer within 30 days. Then Second Appeal with Central/State Information Commissioner. Penalty for delay: ₹250/day on the officer.

💡

RTI is one of the most powerful tools against corruption. Use it freely — officers are legally bound to respond.

Women's Legal Rights
▼
📜
PWDVA 2005 + IPC 498A + POSH Act

Domestic Violence

Domestic violence includes physical, emotional, sexual, and economic abuse. File complaint with Protection Officer (in every district) or directly at police station. Shelter homes available.

✓

Maternity Benefits

Maternity Benefit Act: 26 weeks paid leave for first 2 children, 12 weeks for third. Cannot be dismissed during pregnancy. Right to work from home after return.

✓

Workplace Harassment (POSH)

Every workplace must have Internal Complaints Committee. File complaint within 3 months of incident. Employer must act within 60 days. Confidentiality is mandatory.

✓

Equal Pay

Equal Remuneration Act: women must receive same pay as men for same work. Any discrimination is punishable.

💡

Women's Helpline: 181 | Domestic Violence: 181 | NCW Helpline: 7827-170-170

Rights of Senior Citizens
▼
📜
Maintenance & Welfare of Parents Act 2007

Maintenance from Children

Children (including adopted) are legally obligated to maintain parents and grandparents above 60 years who cannot support themselves. Court can order monthly maintenance.

✓

Property Transfer Reversal

If a senior citizen transferred property to children who later neglect them — the transfer can be revoked by Maintenance Tribunal.

✓

Elder Helpline

National Elder Helpline: 14567 (free). State-run helplines provide legal aid, counseling, and emergency support.

Rights of Transgender Persons
▼
📜
Transgender Persons (Protection of Rights) Act 2019

Key Rights

Right to self-perceived gender identity. Right to non-discrimination in education, employment, healthcare, housing, and public services.

✓

Identity Certificate

Apply for Transgender Identity Certificate through District Magistrate — enables legal recognition for all government documents.

✓

Complaint

Discrimination complaints to National Council for Transgender Persons. Employment discrimination also covered under workplace laws.

🗳️
Right to Vote — Adult Suffrage
▼
1

Secret Ballot is Absolute

No one — not employer, family, party worker — can legally compel you to reveal who you voted for or force you to vote a certain way. Your vote is completely secret.

2

Vote Without Fear

Threatening or bribing a voter is a criminal offence. Report electoral violations to 1950 (ECI helpline) or local Returning Officer.

3

NOTA Option

You can choose NOTA (None of the Above) if you don't want to vote for any candidate. This is a valid and protected choice.

🔒
Rights of Prisoners
▼
1

Basic Rights Remain

Imprisonment restricts liberty — not all rights. Prisoners retain rights to life with dignity, medical care, food, and protection from torture.

2

Right to Meet Lawyer

Prisoners have the right to consult a lawyer at any reasonable time. Jailor cannot permanently block lawyer access.

3

Parole & Furlough

Convicted prisoners are entitled to temporary release (parole for emergency, furlough for maintaining social ties) subject to rules.

🔔
Whistleblower Protection
▼
📜
Whistle Blowers Protection Act 2014

Protection for Exposing Corruption

If you expose corruption by a government employee or department — your identity is protected. Victimization of whistleblowers is illegal.

✓

How to Report

File complaint with Central Vigilance Commission (cvc.gov.in) or Anti-Corruption Bureau of your state. You can file anonymously.

Animal Rights & Cruelty Laws
▼
📜
Prevention of Cruelty to Animals Act 1960

What's Illegal

Beating, kicking, torturing, abandoning, working sick animals, or fighting animals for entertainment — all are criminal offences with fines and imprisonment.

✓

Report Animal Cruelty

Call local SPCA or PFA. File FIR under PCA Act 1960 + IPC 428/429. AWBI (Animal Welfare Board of India) can be contacted at awbi.in.

Labour Rights & Minimum Wage
▼
📜
Minimum Wages Act + Code on Wages 2019

Minimum Wage

Every employer must pay at least the minimum wage set by state/central government for your category of work. Check your state's current minimum wage at labour.gov.in.

✓

Working Hours

Maximum 48 hours/week, 9 hours/day. Overtime must be paid at double rate. Rest of minimum 30 minutes after 5 hours of work.

✓

If Not Paid

File complaint with Labour Commissioner of your district. They have powers to recover unpaid wages. No fee required to file.

💰
PF, Gratuity & Employee Benefits
▼
1

Provident Fund (PF)

Every employer with 20+ employees must contribute to EPF. Both employer and employee contribute 12% of basic salary. Check your UAN on epfindia.gov.in.

2

Gratuity

After 5 years of continuous service — you're entitled to gratuity (15 days salary × years of service). Cannot be denied on resignation or retirement.

3

Unlawful Termination

If terminated without valid reason or without 1 month notice (or pay in lieu) — you can file complaint with Labour Court for reinstatement or compensation.

🤱
Maternity Benefit Act
▼
📜
Maternity Benefit (Amendment) Act 2017

Leave Entitlement

26 weeks paid maternity leave for first 2 children. 12 weeks for 3rd child. Applies to establishments with 10+ employees. Medical bonus also payable if no prenatal care provided.

✓

Cannot Be Dismissed

Employer cannot dismiss or give notice of dismissal during maternity leave. Doing so is illegal and punishable.

✓

Work From Home

After maternity leave, employee can request work-from-home arrangements. Employer must consider it in good faith.

🔐
Right to Privacy & Data Protection
▼
📜
Article 21 + DPDP Act 2023

Privacy as Fundamental Right

Supreme Court ruled (2017) that privacy is a fundamental right. The Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023 now gives you specific digital rights.

✓

Your Data Rights

Right to know what data is collected. Right to correction of wrong data. Right to erase your data (right to be forgotten). Right to withdraw consent.

✓

Data Breach

If a company suffers a data breach affecting you — they must notify you. You can complain to Data Protection Board if your rights are violated.

🗑️
Right to Be Forgotten — Internet
▼
1

What It Covers

You can request removal of personal data, old photos, or private information from Google search results and certain platforms when it's outdated, irrelevant, or violates privacy.

2

Google Removal

Use Google's "Results About You" tool to flag personal info. Submit removal request at support.google.com/websearch.

3

Non-Consensual Images

Intimate images shared without consent: Google, Meta, Microsoft have specific takedown tools. Also complain to cybercrime.gov.in — it's a criminal offence under IT Act.

💻
IT Act & Social Media Rights
▼
1

Section 66A is Dead

The infamous Section 66A (arrest for "offensive" social media posts) was struck down by Supreme Court in 2015. Police cannot arrest you under this section — it no longer exists.

2

Online Defamation

False statements that damage reputation online are actionable. But opinion, satire, and fair comment are protected speech.

3

Platform Accountability

Social media platforms with 5M+ Indian users must appoint a Grievance Officer. Complain to them for content removal. Escalate to cybercrime.gov.in if needed.

Rights of Persons with Disabilities (PwD)
▼
📜
Rights of Persons with Disabilities Act 2016

Recognition

21 types of disabilities are legally recognized — including autism, cerebral palsy, mental illness, acid attack victims, and more.

✓

Reservation

4% reservation in government jobs. 5% reservation in higher education. Priority in government housing. Free travel in some states.

✓

Accessibility Rights

All public buildings, transport, and websites must be made accessible. Non-compliance is punishable. Complain to Chief Commissioner for Persons with Disabilities.

✓

UDID Card

Apply for Unique Disability ID (UDID) at swavlambancard.gov.in — single card for all disability certificates and benefits across India.

⚖️
SC/ST Rights & Atrocities Act
▼
📜
SC/ST (Prevention of Atrocities) Act 1989

Strong Legal Protection

Specific offences against SC/ST members (abuse, forced labor, public humiliation, dispossession from land) carry strict punishment. Non-bailable offences — immediate FIR mandatory.

✓

Special Courts

Atrocity cases are tried in designated Special Courts for faster justice. Victim is entitled to relief and rehabilitation from state even before trial completes.

👶
Child Rights
▼
1

No Child Labour

Children under 14 cannot be employed in any occupation. Children 14–18 cannot work in hazardous industries. Violation: fine + imprisonment for employer.

2

POCSO Protection

Any sexual act with person under 18 is punishable under POCSO — regardless of consent. Penalty: 7 years to life. Report to police or Childline 1098.

3

Child Helpline

Childline: 1098 (free, 24/7) for any child in need — abuse, missing, runaway, labour. NCPCR (National Commission for Protection of Child Rights): ncpcr.gov.in
