Cardiac Arrest
▼
1

Check Response

Tap shoulders, shout "Are you okay?" No response + no normal breathing = cardiac arrest. Call 112 immediately.

2

Start CPR

Place heel of both hands on center of chest. Push hard & fast — at least 5 cm deep, 100–120 times per minute. Let chest fully rise between compressions.

3

Give Rescue Breaths

After 30 compressions: tilt head back, lift chin, pinch nose, give 2 breaths of 1 second each. Chest should visibly rise.

4

Use AED if Available

Switch on AED, attach pads to bare chest as shown. Follow voice instructions. Resume CPR immediately after shock.

5

Keep Going

Don't stop until paramedics arrive or person starts breathing normally. Swap with another person every 2 minutes if possible.

💡

Hum "Stayin' Alive" mentally — it's exactly 100 BPM. Perfect CPR rhythm.

Heart Attack
▼
1

Recognize Signs

Chest pain or tightness, pain spreading to arm/jaw/neck, sweating, nausea, shortness of breath, feeling of doom.

2

Call 112 Immediately

Don't wait. Don't drive yourself. Lie or sit in most comfortable position — usually half-sitting, knees bent.

3

Give Aspirin

If conscious and not allergic — give 300mg aspirin to chew (not swallow whole). This reduces clot damage.

4

Stay Calm & Monitor

Loosen tight clothing. Keep them calm and still. Monitor breathing. If they lose consciousness — start CPR.

⚠️

Heart attack ≠ Cardiac arrest. Heart attack: heart is struggling. Cardiac arrest: heart has stopped. CPR needed only in arrest.

Stroke — FAST Method
▼
F

Face Drooping

Ask to smile — is one side drooping or numb? Uneven smile = warning sign.

A

Arm Weakness

Ask to raise both arms — does one drift downward? Can't hold both up = warning sign.

S

Speech Difficulty

Ask to repeat "The sky is blue" — is speech slurred, strange, or they can't speak? = warning sign.

T

Time to Call 112

If ANY one sign is present — call 112 immediately. Note exact time symptoms started. Brain cells die every minute.

⚠️

Do NOT give food, water, or medicine to a stroke patient. They may have lost ability to swallow safely.

Severe Bleeding
▼
1

Protect Yourself

Wear gloves if available. Use plastic bag or extra cloth to avoid direct blood contact.

2

Apply Direct Pressure

Press firmly with clean cloth. Don't remove — add more on top if soaked. Hold for minimum 10 minutes without lifting.

3

Elevate the Limb

Raise injured arm or leg above heart level. Gravity slows blood flow significantly.

4

Tourniquet if Life-Threatening

Limb only. Tie 5–7 cm above wound using cloth strip. Tighten until bleeding stops. Write time on skin. Loosen after 2 hrs max.

5

Seek Emergency Help

All deep or gushing wounds need hospital. Call 112 if bleeding won't stop after 15 minutes.

⚠️

Never remove an embedded object (glass, knife). Stabilize it in place — removing it can cause massive hemorrhage.

Burns — Thermal, Chemical & Electrical
▼
1

Cool the Burn

Run cool (not ice cold) water over burn for 10–20 minutes. This is the most important step — don't skip it.

2

Remove Clothing & Jewelry

Carefully remove items around burn — but never peel off anything stuck to skin.

3

Cover Loosely

Wrap loosely with clean non-fluffy material or cling film. Don't wrap tight — burns swell.

4

Chemical Burns

Brush off dry chemical first. Then flush with large amounts of water for 20+ minutes. Remove contaminated clothing carefully.

5

Electrical Burns

Disconnect power first. Even small entry wound can have serious internal damage — always get hospital evaluation.

🚫

NEVER: butter, toothpaste, oil, ice, or any cream on burns. They trap heat and cause infection.

💡

Seek emergency help: burns larger than palm, face/hands/genitals burns, blistering, or child burns.

✂️
Amputation
▼
1

Control Bleeding First

Apply tourniquet above amputation site immediately. Press firmly. Time is critical.

2

Preserve the Severed Part

Wrap in clean damp cloth or gauze. Place in clean plastic bag. Then place that bag in ice-cold water. Do NOT put directly on ice.

3

Rush to Hospital

Bring the severed part immediately. Reattachment is possible within 4–6 hours if properly preserved. Call 112.

⚠️

Do not soak the amputated part in water or place directly on ice — it destroys tissue.

👃
Nosebleed
▼
1

Lean Forward

Sit upright, lean slightly forward (not back). Leaning back causes blood to flow into throat — choking risk.

2

Pinch the Soft Part

Pinch the soft lower part of nose (not the bridge). Hold for 10–15 minutes without releasing. Breathe through mouth.

3

Apply Cold Compress

Hold a cold cloth or ice pack on the bridge of nose to help constrict blood vessels.

⚠️

Seek help if: bleeding doesn't stop after 20 minutes, after head injury, or if blood loss is large.

👁️
Eye Injury & Chemical Splash
▼
1

Flush Immediately

For chemical splash: flush with clean water for 20 full minutes. Hold eye open under gentle running water. Don't rub.

2

Foreign Object

Don't rub. Blink rapidly — tear may flush it out. Pull upper lid over lower. If still stuck — cover eye and seek doctor.

3

Penetrating Object

Do NOT remove it. Cover both eyes gently with clean cloth (covering both reduces eye movement). Seek emergency immediately.

⚠️

Never try to remove an object embedded in the eye. You can cause permanent blindness.

🫁
Choking — Heimlich Maneuver
▼
1

Assess First

If they can cough, speak, or breathe — encourage coughing. Don't intervene yet. If they can't — act immediately.

2

5 Back Blows

Lean forward, support chest. Strike firmly between shoulder blades 5 times with heel of palm.

3

5 Abdominal Thrusts

Stand behind, make fist just above navel, grab with other hand. Pull sharply inward and upward 5 times.

4

Repeat & Call 112

Alternate 5 back blows and 5 thrusts until object dislodges or they lose consciousness. If unconscious — start CPR.

⚠️

For infants under 1 year: use 5 back blows + 5 chest thrusts (NOT abdominal thrusts). Never do blind finger sweep.

💨
Asthma Attack
▼
1

Sit Upright

Help them sit upright, leaning slightly forward. Don't lay them down. Loosen tight clothing around chest and neck.

2

Use Inhaler

Give reliever inhaler (usually blue) — 1 puff every 30–60 seconds, up to 10 puffs. Use spacer if available.

3

Stay Calm

Panic worsens breathing. Speak calmly. If no improvement after 10 puffs — call 112 immediately.

4

No Inhaler Available

Sit upright, slow breathing through pursed lips. Remove from triggers (smoke, dust). Call 112 immediately.

⚠️

Call 112 if: lips turn blue, can't complete sentences, no improvement after inhaler, or attack is severe.

🌊
Drowning
▼
1

Get Them Out Safely

Don't jump in unless trained. Throw rope, clothing, or extend a stick. Pull them to safety without putting yourself at risk.

2

Call 112

Call immediately. Even if they seem fine — water in lungs can cause delayed collapse (secondary drowning) hours later.

3

Check Breathing

If not breathing — tilt head back, lift chin, start rescue breathing. Give 5 initial breaths then begin CPR 30:2.

4

Recovery Position

If breathing — turn on side, tilt head back to keep airway open. Keep warm — hypothermia risk after water rescue.

⚠️

Do NOT try to drain water before CPR. Start CPR immediately — it's far more important.

🤧
Allergic Reaction — Anaphylaxis
▼
1

Recognize It Fast

Hives/rash + swelling of face/throat + difficulty breathing + rapid pulse + dizziness = anaphylaxis. Life-threatening. Act NOW.

2

Use EpiPen if Available

Inject into outer thigh (even through clothes). Hold for 10 seconds. Call 112 even after injection — effects last only 15–20 minutes.

3

Lay Them Down

Lie flat with legs raised (unless breathing is difficult — then sit up). Keep still and calm until help arrives.

4

CPR if Needed

If they lose consciousness and stop breathing — begin CPR immediately.

🦴
Fracture & Sprain
▼
1

Stop & Immobilize

Don't try to realign the bone. Stop all movement of the injured area immediately.

2

Splint It

Use rigid material (rolled newspaper, stick, cardboard) padded with cloth on both sides of fracture. Secure above and below injury point.

3

Ice & Elevate

Apply ice wrapped in cloth — not directly on skin. Elevate if limb. This reduces swelling and pain.

4

Seek Help

All suspected fractures need X-ray. Don't let patient walk on a suspected leg fracture. Call 112 for severe cases.

⚠️

Open fracture (bone visible through skin): cover with clean cloth, don't push bone back, call 112 immediately.

🏥
Spinal & Neck Injury
▼
1

Do NOT Move Them

Any movement can cause permanent paralysis. Keep them absolutely still unless there's immediate life threat (fire, water).

2

Stabilize the Head

Hold head gently in position you found it — don't straighten it. Use both hands to keep it absolutely still.

3

Call 112

Describe mechanism of injury. Wait for trained paramedics — they have specialized equipment for safe transport.

⚠️

Suspect spinal injury after: falls from height, high-speed accidents, diving accidents, or unconscious trauma patient.

☀️
Heatstroke / Sunstroke (Lu Lagna)
▼
1

Move to Cool Area

Get them into shade or air-conditioned space immediately. Remove excess clothing.

2

Cool the Body Fast

Apply wet cloth to neck, armpits, and groin. Fan them. Use ice packs wrapped in cloth. Sponge with cool water.

3

Hydrate if Conscious

If conscious and can swallow — give water or ORS (not very cold). Never force fluids on unconscious person.

4

Call 112

Heatstroke (not heat exhaustion) is life-threatening. Confusion, no sweating, temp above 40°C = emergency.

⚠️

Heat exhaustion: heavy sweating, weakness. Heatstroke: hot dry skin, confusion — far more dangerous. Both need cooling, heatstroke needs 112.

🥶
Frostbite & Hypothermia
▼
1

Move to Warmth

Get to a warm, dry shelter. Remove wet clothing. Wrap in blankets, especially around core, head, and neck.

2

Rewarm Frostbitten Area

Soak in warm (not hot) water 37–40°C for 15–30 minutes. Stop when skin turns pink and feeling returns.

3

Do NOT Rub

Never rub frostbitten skin — ice crystals inside can tear tissue. Handle gently. Cover with sterile bandage.

4

Give Warm Drinks

If conscious — warm (not hot) sweet drinks. No alcohol. Call 112 for severe hypothermia (unconscious, shivering stopped).

🐍
Snake Bite
▼
1

Stay Calm & Keep Still

Movement speeds venom spread. Sit or lie down. Keep bitten limb below heart level. Do NOT run.

2

Remove Constrictions

Remove rings, watches, tight clothing near bite — swelling will come fast.

3

Call 112 & Note the Snake

If safe, take a photo or remember color/pattern — helps doctors give correct antivenom. Don't try to catch it.

4

Rush to Hospital

Antivenom is the only real treatment. Get there as fast as possible. Keep patient still during transport.

🚫

NEVER: cut the wound, suck venom, apply tourniquet, apply ice. These are dangerous myths.

🐕
Dog & Monkey Bites — Rabies Prevention
▼
1

Wash Wound Immediately

Wash with soap and running water for 15 minutes minimum. This is the most important step — reduces rabies risk significantly.

2

Apply Antiseptic

Apply povidone-iodine or 70% alcohol after washing. Cover with clean bandage.

3

Get Anti-Rabies Vaccine

Go to hospital within 24 hours. 5-dose vaccine schedule: Day 0, 3, 7, 14, 28. Don't skip doses — rabies is fatal once symptoms appear.

4

RIG if Deep Wound

For deep/multiple bites — doctor may give Rabies Immunoglobulin (RIG) at wound site on Day 0. Very important.

⚠️

Rabies is 100% fatal once symptoms appear. Vaccine after bite works — but only if given before symptoms start.

🐝
Insect Bites & Bee Stings
▼
1

Remove Stinger

Scrape stinger out sideways with a card or fingernail — don't pinch/squeeze (releases more venom).

2

Wash & Cool

Wash with soap and water. Apply ice pack for 10–15 minutes to reduce pain and swelling.

3

Monitor for Allergy

Watch for 30 minutes: hives, swelling spreading, difficulty breathing = anaphylaxis emergency. Call 112.

💡

Multiple bee stings (50+) can cause toxic reaction even without allergy. Always seek medical help for mass stings.

🐾
Pet / Stray Animal First Aid
▼
1

Approach Safely

An injured animal may bite out of fear. Approach slowly, speak softly. Use a cloth or jacket to cover the animal's head first.

2

Control Bleeding

Apply gentle pressure with clean cloth on wounds. Don't use elastic bands — can cut off circulation.

3

Don't Feed or Give Water

Injured animals may vomit or have internal injuries. No food or water until vet evaluation.

4

Transport to Vet

Carry in a box lined with cloth. Call local animal helpline (PFA: 1962 in many cities). Keep animal warm and calm.

☠️
Poisoning & Overdose
▼
1

Call 112 First

Note what was ingested, how much, and when. Tell this to 112 — they can advise over the phone.

2

Do NOT Induce Vomiting

Unless specifically told by doctor/poison control. For corrosives (acids, bleach) — vomiting causes more damage coming back up.

3

If Unconscious

Place in recovery position. Keep airway clear. Do not give anything by mouth. Monitor breathing.

4

Bring the Container

Take the medicine bottle, plant, or substance to hospital — doctors need to identify the exact poison.

⚠️

For alcohol/drug overdose: don't let them "sleep it off" alone. Monitor continuously — people die from airway obstruction.

🩺
Diabetic Emergency
▼
1

Low Blood Sugar (Hypoglycemia)

Signs: shaking, sweating, confusion, pale, fast heartbeat. Give: sugar, juice, glucose tablet, or sweet drink immediately if conscious.

2

High Blood Sugar (Hyperglycemia)

Signs: extreme thirst, frequent urination, fruity breath, confusion. Give water if conscious. Needs hospital for insulin — don't delay.

3

If Unconscious

Do NOT give anything by mouth — choking risk. Recovery position. Call 112. Don't leave alone.

💡

When unsure: give sugar anyway. Giving sugar to a high-sugar patient is less dangerous than not giving it to a low-sugar patient.

🌡️
High Fever in Children
▼
1

Measure Temperature

Above 38°C is fever. Above 40°C is high fever — needs immediate action. Above 41°C = emergency.

2

Cool the Child

Remove excess clothing. Sponge with lukewarm (not cold) water on forehead, armpits, neck. Fan gently.

3

Give Fever Medicine

Paracetamol (Calpol) at correct weight-based dose. Give fluids — breast milk, water, ORS. Never give aspirin to children.

4

Febrile Seizure

If child shakes/convulses: lay on side, don't restrain, don't put anything in mouth, clear area. Call 112. Usually stops in 1–2 min.

🩸
Vomiting or Spitting Blood
▼
1

Call 112 Immediately

Vomiting blood is always serious. It indicates internal bleeding — upper digestive tract, stomach ulcer, or serious injury.

2

Recovery Position

Lay on left side to prevent choking. Keep airway clear. Don't give food or water.

3

Monitor & Reassure

Keep calm. Note time, amount, and color of blood — this helps doctors. Stay with patient until help arrives.

⚡
Seizure / Convulsion (Mirgi)
▼
1

Stay Calm & Time It

Note exact time seizure started. Most end in 1–3 minutes on their own. Stay close and watch.

2

Protect From Injury

Clear hard/sharp objects away. Place something soft under the head. Do NOT restrain their movements.

3

Do NOT Put Anything in Mouth

Old myth — people cannot swallow their tongue. Putting objects in mouth causes broken teeth, injured jaw, and injury to helper.

4

After Seizure

Turn on side (recovery position). They may be confused for minutes — speak gently. Stay with them until fully alert.

⚠️

Call 112 if: seizure lasts more than 5 minutes, person doesn't regain consciousness, multiple seizures occur, or it's a first-ever seizure.

😵
Fainting & Unconsciousness
▼
1

If They Feel Faint

Sit or lie down immediately. Legs up above heart level. Loosen tight clothing. Move to cool, fresh air area.

2

If They've Collapsed

Check response — call name, tap shoulder. Open airway — tilt head back, lift chin. Check breathing for 10 seconds.

3

Recovery Position

If breathing — roll onto left side, tilt head back to maintain airway. This prevents choking if they vomit.

4

Not Breathing — CPR

If no breathing after head tilt — begin CPR immediately. Call 112.

😰
Panic Attack
▼
1

Reassure Them

Say clearly: "You are safe. This will pass. I'm here with you." Panic attacks feel like dying but are not physically dangerous.

2

Breathing Technique

Guide: breathe in slowly for 4 counts, hold for 4, out for 4. Repeat. This physically calms the nervous system.

3

Grounding — 5-4-3-2-1

Ask: name 5 things you see, 4 you can touch, 3 you hear, 2 you smell, 1 you taste. Brings focus back to present.

4

Don't Leave Them Alone

Stay calm yourself — anxiety is contagious. Remove from crowded or stimulating environment if possible.

💡

Don't say "calm down" or "it's nothing." Say "I'm here, you're safe, this will pass" — much more effective.

🧠
Suicidal Ideation / Mental Health Crisis
▼
1

Take It Seriously

Never dismiss or say "you're just seeking attention." Any mention of suicide must be taken seriously. Your response matters.

2

Ask Directly

It is safe to ask: "Are you thinking about ending your life?" Asking does not plant the idea — it opens a door for them to speak.

3

Listen Without Judging

Don't offer quick solutions. Don't say "you have so much to live for." Just listen and stay present. Your presence is the help.

4

Connect to Help

iCall: 9152987821 | Vandrevala Foundation: 1860-2662-345 (24/7) | NIMHANS: 080-46110007. Stay with them until help arrives.

⚠️

If immediate danger: remove access to means (medicines, sharp objects). Don't leave alone. Call 112 if needed.

💭
PTSD Flashback — Grounding Someone
▼
1

Stay Calm Yourself

Speak in a low, steady voice. Don't touch without permission — sudden touch can worsen the flashback. Announce your actions.

2

Orient to Present

Say: "You are safe. You are here with me in [place]. It is [year]. What you're experiencing is a memory — not happening now."

3

Grounding Technique

Guide them: feel your feet on the floor, feel the chair, name 5 things in this room. Physical sensations pull them back to present.

4

After They're Grounded

Don't ask about what happened. Offer water, sit with them. Encourage professional support if episodes are recurring.

⚡
Electric Shock First Aid
▼
1

Don't Touch the Victim

You will be shocked too. Disconnect power at mains switch first. Use dry wooden stick or rubber to push wire away if needed.

2

Check & Start CPR

Once safe — check breathing. If not breathing — start CPR immediately. Electric shock can stop the heart.

3

Treat Burns

Electric burns have entry and exit wounds — check both. Cool burn areas with water. Cover with clean cloth.

4

Always Seek Hospital

Even if the person seems fine — internal heart rhythm damage can cause delayed collapse. Always get evaluated.

🌫️
Smoke Inhalation
▼
1

Move to Fresh Air

Get away from smoke immediately. Stay low — smoke rises. Fresh air is the first treatment.

2

Loosen Clothing

Loosen anything around neck and chest. Sit upright. If unconscious — recovery position and call 112.

3

Watch for Delayed Symptoms

Coughing, hoarse voice, breathing difficulty may worsen hours later. Any smoke inhalation = hospital check.
