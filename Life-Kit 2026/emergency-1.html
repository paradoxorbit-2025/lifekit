🏚️
Earthquake
▼
1

DROP to the Ground

Don't run. Drop to hands and knees immediately — prevents being knocked down. Get under sturdy table if nearby.

2

COVER Head & Neck

Use arms to protect head and neck. Stay away from windows, walls, and heavy furniture like bookshelves.

3

HOLD ON Until Shaking Stops

Stay in position. Shaking usually lasts under 60 seconds but feels longer. Don't move until it stops completely.

4

Evacuate Carefully

Check for injuries, watch for broken glass and debris. Exit using stairs only — never lifts. Move to open ground.

5

Expect Aftershocks

Stay away from damaged buildings. Don't enter collapsed structures. Stay away from trees and power lines.

⚠️

Doorway myth is wrong — a sturdy table offers far better protection than a doorway.

🌊
Flood & Heavy Rainfall
▼
1

Move to High Ground Immediately

Don't wait for official orders. Go to upper floors or elevated areas. Floodwater rises faster than expected.

2

Never Walk Through Floodwater

Even 15 cm of moving water can knock you down. Water may carry sewage, chemicals, or be electrified by downed lines.

3

Turn Off Electricity

Switch off main power at fuse box before water enters if safe to do so. Electrocution is a major flood risk.

4

Emergency Kit Ready

Aadhar, medicines, drinking water, torch, ORS, warm clothes. Have this bag packed before monsoon season.

5

Signal for Rescue

If trapped — signal from roof or window with bright cloth. Call 112. Don't swim in fast-moving water.

💡

Boil all water for at least 2 minutes after floods — taps may be contaminated for days after water recedes.

🌀
Tsunami & Cyclone
▼
1

Tsunami Warning Signs

Sea suddenly pulls back (unusual low tide), strong earthquake felt near coast, loud roaring sound from ocean. Evacuate immediately inland — don't wait for official warning.

2

Tsunami: Move Inland Fast

Go to high ground (at least 30m above sea level) or at least 3 km inland. Stay until all-clear from authorities.

3

Cyclone: Stay Indoors

Get inside strong building. Stay away from windows and doors. Go to lowest floor — not top floor. Fill water containers before power goes.

4

Cyclone Eye — Don't Go Out

The sudden calm in middle of cyclone is the "eye." Wind will return violently in minutes. Stay inside.

⛰️
Landslide
▼
1

Warning Signs

Cracks in ground, tilting trees, unusual sounds (rumbling), sudden muddy water in streams. Evacuate immediately.

2

Move Perpendicular to Slide

Run sideways — not uphill or downhill. A landslide travels extremely fast in its direct path.

3

If Caught

Curl into ball, protect head. Try to grab onto stable tree or rock. Avoid river channels and low areas.

⚡
Lightning Strike
▼
1

Get Inside

Seek shelter in solid building or hard-topped vehicle immediately. Lightning can strike from 10+ km away from storm center.

2

If Caught Outside

Lightning Squat position: crouch low, feet together, heels raised, cover ears. Don't lie flat. Move away from tall trees, water, and metal.

3

If Someone is Struck

It is safe to touch them — lightning doesn't stay in body. Check breathing, start CPR if needed. Call 112.

⚠️

30-30 rule: if thunder comes within 30 seconds of lightning — get inside. Wait 30 minutes after last thunder before going out.

🔥
Fire Outbreak — Building
▼
1

Alert & Get Out

Shout "Fire!" loudly. Activate fire alarm if available. Leave immediately — don't waste time grabbing belongings.

2

Stay Low Under Smoke

Smoke rises — crawl on hands and knees. Cover nose and mouth with wet cloth. Keep eyes low.

3

Check Door Before Opening

Feel door with back of hand. If hot — don't open. Use another exit or signal from window for rescue.

4

Never Use Lifts

Always use stairs. Lifts can malfunction or open onto fire floor.

5

Call 101 & Assemble

Meet at pre-decided point outside. Call 101 (Fire) or 112. Never re-enter burning building.

⚠️

Clothes on fire: STOP, DROP, ROLL. Running fans the flames.

🧯
Fire Extinguisher — PASS Method
▼
P

Pull the Pin

Pull the safety pin at the top of the extinguisher. This unlocks the handle.

A

Aim at Base

Point the nozzle at the BASE of the fire — not the flames. This cuts the fuel source.

S

Squeeze Handle

Squeeze the handle firmly and evenly to discharge. Keep squeezing continuously.

S

Sweep Side to Side

Move nozzle from side to side covering entire base of fire. Continue until fire is out.

💡

Only fight fire if: it's small, you have a clear exit behind you, and you know the extinguisher type. Never fight large fires — escape first.

🏨
Hotel Fire Evacuation
▼
1

On Check-In

Immediately locate nearest fire exit from your room. Count the doors — you may need to crawl in smoke and count by touch.

2

Keep Room Key Card

Take your key card when evacuating. If corridors are smoky — you may need to return to room and shelter in place.

3

Shelter in Room if Needed

If corridor is filled with smoke: stay in room, seal gaps under door with wet towels, call front desk and 101. Signal from window.

🌲
Wildfire / Forest Fire
▼
1

Evacuate Immediately

Don't underestimate wildfire speed — it can outrun a person. Leave when warned, not when you see flames.

2

Move Against Wind

Fire travels in the direction of wind. Move perpendicular or against wind direction.

3

If Surrounded

Find lowest ground with least vegetation. Lie face down in a depression or ditch. Cover body with soil. Protect airway.

🚗
Brake Failure
▼
1

Don't Panic

Grip steering wheel firmly. Keep looking ahead. Panic causes accidents more than brake failure itself.

2

Downshift Gears

Shift to lower gears immediately — this slows the vehicle using engine braking. Manual: go down to 2nd then 1st. Auto: use L or 1.

3

Pump the Brakes

Pump brake pedal rapidly — this may build enough pressure for partial braking in hydraulic systems.

4

Handbrake Slowly

Apply handbrake gradually — not suddenly — or vehicle will skid. Use to bring speed down slowly.

5

Find Escape Route

Aim for uphill road, gravel, bushes, or open field to slow down. Honk continuously to warn others.

🚙
Car Sinking in Water
▼
1

Stay Calm — You Have Time

Car takes 60–120 seconds to sink fully. Don't waste those seconds panicking. Act systematically.

2

Seatbelt Off First

Unbuckle seatbelt immediately. Don't wait. If jammed — cut with sharp object or window smasher.

3

Open Window Immediately

Open window as soon as car hits water — electronics work for ~30 seconds. This is your exit. Don't open door yet.

4

Break Window if Needed

Use headrest spike or heel of shoe at corner of window. Center of window is strongest — always hit corner.

5

Wait for Pressure to Equalize

If door won't open — wait until inside water level equals outside, then door will open. Take a deep breath and exit fast.

💡

Keep a window smasher/seatbelt cutter tool in your car's glove box. Under ₹200 — potentially life-saving.

🚑
Road Accident — As a Witness
▼
1

Secure the Scene

Park safely. Turn on hazard lights. Place warning triangle or have someone wave off traffic from 50m away.

2

Call 112 Immediately

Give exact location, number of casualties, type of vehicle. Don't hang up — stay on line.

3

Don't Move Casualties

Unless immediate danger (fire, water). Moving someone with spinal injury can cause paralysis. Wait for paramedics.

4

You're Protected by Good Samaritan Law

Indian law protects bystanders who help accident victims. You cannot be questioned, detained, or harassed by police for helping.

💨
Gas Leak (LPG/CNG)
▼
1

Don't Touch Any Switch

Even a light switch can spark. Don't switch on or off anything electrical — spark can ignite gas.

2

Open All Windows & Doors

Ventilate immediately. Don't use fans — they're electrical. Open manually. Move away from gas smell area.

3

Turn Off Cylinder Valve

Turn the knob on top of cylinder clockwise to close. Only if cylinder is accessible without going near flames.

4

Exit & Call Help

Evacuate all people. Call from outside — 101 (Fire) or your gas supplier. Don't re-enter until cleared.

🚨
Terror Attack / Active Shooter
▼
R

RUN

If there's an escape route — run. Leave belongings. Help others if safe to do so. Don't wait for others who won't come.

H

HIDE

If you can't run — hide. Lock/barricade door. Silence phone. Turn off lights. Stay away from doors and windows.

T

TELL

Call 100 (Police) when safe. Whisper location, description of attacker, number of shots heard, your location.

F

FIGHT (Last Resort)

If cornered with no option — fight back aggressively. Use any object as weapon. Commit fully — half-hearted resistance is worse.

🏗️
Building Collapse
▼
1

Drop & Cover Immediately

Get under desk or sturdy table. Protect head and neck. Stay away from exterior walls and windows.

2

Triangle of Life Spots

Next to (not under) sturdy furniture — when ceiling collapses on object, a void (triangle) forms beside it. This can save you.

3

If Trapped

Don't shout continuously — wastes energy. Tap on pipe or wall to signal rescuers. Shout only when you hear someone nearby.

4

Dust & Debris

Cover nose with cloth. Breathe shallowly. Concrete dust inhalation is dangerous.

👥
Stampede
▼
1

Don't Fight the Crowd

Move with the crowd initially. Fighting it causes injury. Wait for an opportunity to edge sideways toward a wall or edge.

2

Protect Your Chest Space

Fold arms in front of chest (like boxing guard). This creates a breathing space even when crushed from all sides.

3

If You Fall

Curl into ball, protect head and chest, get up immediately — being on ground is most dangerous. Use your legs to push up.

4

Move Diagonally to Exit

Once you find space — move diagonally toward edge or exit. Never go against crowd flow directly.

☢️
Industrial / Chemical Disaster
▼
1

Move Against Wind

Toxic gases travel with wind. Move perpendicular or upwind from the source. Don't run downwind.

2

Cover Nose & Mouth

Use wet cloth as improvised filter. N95 mask if available. Don't use regular surgical masks — they don't filter chemicals.

3

Shelter in Place if Ordered

If authorities say shelter in place: seal doors and windows with tape and wet cloth. Turn off HVAC. Wait for all-clear.

☢️
Nuclear / Radiation Alert
▼
1

Follow Official Instructions ONLY

Misinformation spreads fast. Follow only government/civil defense broadcasts. Don't act on rumors.

2

Get Inside

Concrete/brick buildings block radiation. Go inside, close windows and doors, turn off HVAC. Distance and shielding are protection.

3

Potassium Iodide if Issued

Take KI tablets only if distributed by health authorities — they protect the thyroid gland only from radioactive iodine. Take correct dose.

⚠️

Do not take Potassium Iodide without official instruction — it has serious side effects and doesn't protect against all radiation.

🥵
Extreme Heatwave (45°C+)
▼
1

Stay Indoors 12–4 PM

Peak heat hours. If you must go out — cotton/light clothes, hat, sunglasses, water. Never leave children or elderly alone indoors without ventilation.

2

Hydrate Constantly

Drink water even if not thirsty — thirst lags behind dehydration. ORS or lemon salt water better than plain water in extreme heat.

3

Power Grid Failure

Keep phone charged before outage. Wet sheet + fan creates cooling even in power cut. Identify nearest cooling center (government hospitals, malls).

4

Check on Vulnerable People

Elderly, infants, outdoor workers are at highest risk. Check on neighbors. Heatstroke can be silent until collapse.

😷
Toxic Smog / Air Pollution Emergency
▼
1

Check AQI Before Going Out

AQI above 200 = very unhealthy. Above 300 = hazardous. Use SAFAR app (India) or IQAir for real-time reading.

2

Use N95/N99 Mask

Regular surgical masks don't filter PM2.5 particles. Only N95 or better provides real protection. Fit tightly — no gaps.

3

Stay Indoors

Keep windows closed during peak pollution. Wet mopping reduces indoor dust. Indoor plants help marginally — don't rely on them alone.

4

At-Risk Groups

Asthma, heart patients, children, elderly must avoid all outdoor activity above AQI 150. Keep inhalers accessible.

❄️
Blizzard / Extreme Cold
▼
1

Stay Inside

Don't travel in blizzard conditions unless emergency. More people die of exposure trying to travel than staying put.

2

Carbon Monoxide Warning

Never use generators, grills, or kerosene heaters inside — they produce deadly CO gas. Colorless, odorless, kills silently. Keep one window slightly open.

3

If Stranded in Vehicle

Stay in car — it's shelter. Run engine 10 minutes per hour for warmth. Keep window cracked for ventilation. Clear exhaust pipe of snow.

🔌
City-Wide Blackout / Cyber Failure
▼
1

Download Offline Maps Now

Before emergency: download Google Maps or Maps.me for your city offline. Works without internet — essential during blackouts.

2

Keep Cash Ready

ATMs and digital payments fail in blackouts. Always keep ₹500–2000 cash at home for emergencies.

3

Power Bank & Candles

Keep a charged power bank and candles/torch at home always. Recharge power bank every month even if unused.

🚸
Child Missing
▼
1

Search Immediately — Don't Wait

Search nearby area for 5 minutes. Call out their name. Check all bathrooms, corners, under furniture — first.

2

Alert Security/Staff

If in a mall, station, or public place — alert security immediately. They have protocols and cameras.

3

Call 112 / Childline 1098

File a missing person report immediately — no waiting period required for children. Childline: 1098 (free, 24/7).

4

Share Photo Immediately

Have a recent photo on your phone always. Share with police, WhatsApp groups, and station CCTV teams immediately.

✈️
Lost Passport / Documents Abroad
▼
1

File Police Report First

Go to local police station and file FIR. This report is essential for emergency travel document from Indian Embassy.

2

Contact Indian Embassy / Consulate

Find nearest Indian Embassy. Carry: police report, any ID proof, passport-size photos. They can issue Emergency Certificate for return travel.

3

Contact Bank

Block cards immediately via app or call. International transfers via Western Union/MoneyGram if completely stranded.

4

Prevention

Always keep: digital copies on email/cloud, photocopy of passport in luggage, note of embassy number before traveling.

Emergency Kit — Home Checklist
▼
✓

Documents

Photocopies of Aadhar, PAN, passport, bank passbook, insurance. Keep in waterproof zip-lock bag.

✓

Water & Food

3-day supply: 4 litres water per person per day, dry food (biscuits, chikki, energy bars, ORS packets).

✓

First Aid

Bandages, antiseptic, pain relievers (paracetamol), ORS, scissors, gloves, personal medicines (minimum 1 week supply).

✓

Tools & Power

Torch + extra batteries, power bank (charged), whistle, portable radio (battery), cash ₹2000+, lighter/matches.

✓

Contacts List

Written (not just on phone) list: family numbers, doctor, neighbor, local police station, nearest hospital.

💡

Check and refresh this kit every 6 months. Replace expired food, medicines, and recharge power bank.
